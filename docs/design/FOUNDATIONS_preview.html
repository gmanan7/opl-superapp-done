01 The feel statement

A calm instrument for people
who have no time to spare.

Fulcrum is held one-handed over a running machine, and it is also projected in a leadership review. The same surface has to feel quiet under pressure and trustworthy under scrutiny. That is the whole brief: an instrument, not a website.

If you remember one thing, remember this: the task is the interface. An operator entering yesterday's production should never look at our design — they should look through it at the number they came to enter. Decoration that slows a capture by even a second is a defect, because the only competitor we have is paper, and paper is instant.

Six adjectives — and what each forbids

Calm
Warm-neutral surfaces, one accent, generous whitespace. The screen rests until you act.
Forbids: gradient fills, decorative color, busy borders.
Direct
The next action is obvious and within thumb's reach. One primary per screen.
Forbids: two amber buttons competing on one view.
Legible
Big where it's read in motion; dense only where it's studied at a desk.
Forbids: body text below 14px, ever; capture text below 16px.
Equal
Tamil looks as deliberate as English. No script is an afterthought.
Forbids: a Latin-only display face; truncated translated labels.
Sturdy
Sober weight, real contrast, factory-floor durable. It reads in glare and gloves.
Forbids: thin hairline type, low-contrast grays as text.
Honest
Skeletons not spinners; missing data is named, not hidden behind a blank chart.
Forbids: layout shift on load; fake-empty states.

The eight laws (pin these above every future screen)

L1
Faster than paper, or it doesn't ship.
Every capture flow is timed against the format it replaces. If it loses, it is redesigned — not decorated. This is law #1 because it is the product's survival condition.
L2
Amber is a scalpel, not a highlighter.
The accent marks exactly one thing per view: the action you came to take. When everything is amber, nothing is. Surfaces stay warm-neutral; the accent earns attention by being rare.
L3
Every glyph is first-class.
A screen in Tamil must look as composed as the same screen in English — same rhythm, same weight, no clipped matras, no overflowing buttons. We design to the longest language, never the shortest.
L4
Status is felt in three channels.
Color + symbol + label, always together. A colour-blind leader on a glare-lit floor must read a state from its shape and its word alone.
L5
Big where the thumb lands.
Capture targets are ≥44px and bottom-anchored for one-handed reach. Analysis surfaces may be dense — generosity and density are both correct, in their own place.
L6
Numbers hold still.
KPI figures are tabular, lining, Latin-numeral, right-aligned. A form never reflows while a thumb is mid-entry; a chart never jumps when its data arrives.
L7
The original is never destroyed.
Translation is a toggle laid over submitted content, not a replacement of it. A worker's Gujarati words are always one tap away, intact.
L8
Earn every pixel of chrome.
If an element doesn't help capture or judgment, it's removed. No filler tiles, no vanity stats, no decoration masquerading as polish.
02 How the identity evolves

Keep the character.
Sharpen everything else.

The prototype's instinct was right — warm, dark-sidebar, amber-accented, shadcn-based. It was just thin: no hierarchy of surface, an accent that doubles as a warning, a type stack that only truly serves 20% of users. We deepen it; we don't restart.

● Keep

  • Dark warm sidebarThe stone-900 rail is distinctive and orients leaders. Stays — retuned to #1A1714.
  • Warm accentAmber is the brand's warmth. Stays as the single accent hue.
  • shadcn/ui + TailwindDelivery mechanism. Tokens map onto it; they never fight it.
  • Stone neutral familyWarm grays beat cool grays for a human, on-floor tool.

◑ Sharpen

  • Amber → burnt amber#D97706 hot-orange deepens to #B45309 so white text passes AA. More confident, more accessible.
  • One surface → fourbase / raised / sunken / inverse give real depth and rhythm instead of flat white-on-white.
  • Accent vs warning splitBrand = burnt-orange; warning = ochre #A16207. They stop colliding.
  • Status → 3-channelEvery state gets a symbol + label, not just a color.

✕ Change out

  • DM Sans as the type voiceA Latin-only display face creates a two-tier identity. Replaced by one unified Noto family across all four scripts.
  • Color-only statesThe red/blue/green/amber-by-hue approach fails the colour-blind floor test. Retired.
  • Flat elevationBorderless white cards on white. Replaced by a warm-tinted shadow + line system.
  • Hot-orange CTA on whiteFails contrast at body size. Retired in favour of the deeper accent.
🎯

The evolution in one line: same warm, sober, amber-and-stone character the prototype reached for — now given a real surface hierarchy, an accessible accent, a colour-blind-safe status system, and a type stack that treats all four scripts as one voice.

03 Type & font implementation

One family. Four scripts.
No second-class glyphs.

The decision: pure Noto Sans across all four scripts — Noto Sans (Latin) + Noto Sans Devanagari + Gujarati + Tamil — bound into a single CSS family. We do not pair DM Sans for Latin. Here's the reasoning, shown not asserted.

Why not DM Sans (Latin) + Noto (Indic)?

The brief allows the pairing "if baseline/weight harmony is demonstrated." When you put them side by side, it isn't there. DM Sans is a geometric display face with a tall x-height and closed apertures; Noto's Indic faces are humanist, designed by the same team that drew Noto Sans Latin to share vertical metrics and weight axis. Pairing means a geometric Latin sitting next to a humanist Devanagari — two voices — and worse, it makes the language you ship in change the typeface you see. For an app whose 80% read Indic scripts, that is a two-tier identity by construction.

✓ Noto Sans across both

Shared metrics, one weight system, identical numerals. The two scripts read as one typeface — because they are one type system.
💡

Distinctiveness doesn't come from the typeface. It comes from the system — the burnt amber, the surface hierarchy, the scale rhythm, the dark rail. A display face that only works in one of four scripts is a liability we don't need to take on. Noto gives us unbreakable harmony; the brand gives us the character.

Implementation spec — code-ready

DecisionSpecification
FamilyNoto Sans (Latin) · Noto Sans Devanagari · Noto Sans Gujarati · Noto Sans Tamil · Noto Sans Mono (data/code). Bound as one logical family, Fulcrum Sans.
Weights400 Regular · 500 Medium · 600 Semibold · 700 Bold. Same four weights in every script (Noto Indic ships them). No 300/light — fails floor legibility.
NumeralsLatin / Western-Arabic everywhere (21,480, never २१,४८०). Enforce font-variant-numeric: lining-nums tabular-nums on all KPI & table figures so columns align and don't reflow.
SubsetsPer script: latin, latin-ext, devanagari, gujarati, tamil. Self-host woff2, one file per script × weight. No CJK, no unused subsets.
LoadingSelf-hosted woff2 with font-display: swap. <link rel="preload"> only Noto Sans 400 + 600 Latin and the active language's 400 + 600 (injected by locale). Lazy-load the rest.
BindingOne @font-face per script scoped by unicode-range, all named "Fulcrum Sans". The browser picks the right script per codepoint automatically — app code only ever writes font-family: "Fulcrum Sans".
Line-heightBody ≥ 1.6, headings ≥ 1.25. Indic stacking marks (Devanagari matras, Tamil/Gujarati loops) clip below 1.5 — the scale below bakes this in. Never set a fixed pixel line-height on multilingual text.
Fallback chainLatin → system-ui, -apple-system, "Segoe UI", Roboto. Devanagari → "Nirmala UI", sans-serif. Gujarati → "Nirmala UI", "Shruti". Tamil → "Nirmala UI", "Latha". Each scoped in its own @font-face fallback so swap never shows tofu.

05 Status system

Color + symbol + label.
Never color alone.

A colour-blind reviewer under factory glare must decode a state from its shape and its word, not its hue. Every status token ships as a triple. Warning is ochre, deliberately split from the burnt-amber brand accent so the two never read as the same signal.

⚠️

Implementation rule for Claude Code: a <StatusBadge> takes status + a translated label and always renders symbol + label together. There is no color-only variant in the API — it cannot be misused.

08 Where the brief under-specifies · tensions

Open questions & the
tensions I resolved.

Questions for the owner

1
Dark mode? The floor is often bright (daylight, glare) but some lines run night shifts. Do we owe a dark theme in Phase 1–2, or is it a later token-flip? My tokens are structured to support it (semantic surfaces), but I haven't drawn the inverse ramp yet. Recommend: defer, but reserve surface-inverse semantics now so we don't repaint later.
2
Numerals in body prose vs data. Brief mandates Latin numerals for KPI legibility — confirmed. But in translated prose (a Hindi OPL remark), should an inline quantity also force Latin, or follow the sentence? I've assumed Latin everywhere for consistency. Confirm this is acceptable to native readers, or we scope it to data contexts only.
3
Brand wordmark. Is "Fulcrum" / "TPM Fulcrum" final, and is there an existing logo lockup I should tokenize around? The cover treats it as a wordmark + amber mark placeholder. If a real mark exists, send it and I'll fold it into the shell spec next session.
4
Print typeface. Constraint #7 says print views (OPL/Kaizen A4) must match factory PPT formats. Those formats may mandate a specific font (often Calibri/Arial). Do print stylesheets stay on Fulcrum Sans, or match the legacy PPT font for continuity? Recommend: Fulcrum Sans for brand coherence unless a format is contractually fixed.
5
Self-hosting weight budget. 4 scripts × 4 weights × woff2 ≈ 16+ files. On a low-end shop-floor phone over patchy networks, is the full 700-weight set worth it, or do we cut Indic to 400/600 only and synthesize emphasis? Recommend: ship 400/600 per Indic script first, add 500/700 if needed — keeps the critical path light.

Tensions I found — named, not silently picked

Amber as brand vs amber as warning

The kept accent (amber) collides with the universal convention that amber/yellow means "caution." If both are amber, a primary button and a warning badge read as the same signal.

Resolved: brand accent is burnt orange (#B45309); warning status is ochre/yellow (#A16207) on its own tint. Distinct hues, plus warning always carries the ▲ symbol + label. The accent lives on interactive controls; warning lives in badges — they never occupy the same role.
Faster-than-paper vs WCAG AA contrast

The prototype's bright orange CTA (#D97706 + white) is punchy and fast to spot — but fails AA at body text size (3.2:1). Accessibility and "instantly findable primary" pull against each other.

Resolved: deepen the accent to #B45309 (white text = 5.0:1, passes AA), and keep #D97706 as accent-bright only for large fills/chart marks where AA-large (3:1) is the bar. We get both: a findable CTA and a compliant one.
Distinctive type vs four-script harmony

Brief §2 wants more "distinctiveness"; keeping DM Sans for Latin would add character. But DM Sans only renders Latin — so the typeface would change with the language, breaking harmony for 80% of users (constraint #2).

Resolved in favour of harmony: one Noto family across all scripts. Distinctiveness is carried by the system — burnt amber, surface depth, scale rhythm, the dark rail — not by a display face that abandons three of our four scripts.
Capture generosity vs leader density

≥44px targets and 16px-minimum text make capture fast on a phone — but the People roster and KPI grid need density to be useful to a reviewer at a desk. One size can't serve both.

Resolved: the system is dual-mode by surface, not one compromise. Capture surfaces use touch spacing + base↑ type; analysis surfaces may drop to sm type and tighter rows. Same tokens, two density profiles — codified so it's a choice, not a drift.
